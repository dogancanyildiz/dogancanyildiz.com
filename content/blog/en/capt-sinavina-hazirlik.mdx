## What the exam measures

CAPT tests the hands-on part of penetration testing, not memorisation. You need to profile a target, validate a finding and explain why it matters, all three at once. Most of my preparation time went into the lab, not into reading.

## How I studied

I started by listing gaps and splitting topics into two buckets: things I had never touched and things I only knew superficially. I rebuilt the first group from scratch and rehearsed the second in vulnerable environments I stood up myself. Running my own Docker lab mattered more than I expected, because a one-click lab never shows the setup and reconnaissance time a real target costs you.

## What I over-studied

Reporting. Finding a vulnerability is half the job; writing it so the other side understands it is the other half. I started keeping notes under three headings every time: what I found, how I verified it and what should change. That habit helped in the exam and afterwards.

## What comes next

The certificate is a starting point, not a finish line. My next step is to apply what I learned systematically in the applications I build; the ticket purchasing project on this site was the first deliberate attempt.
